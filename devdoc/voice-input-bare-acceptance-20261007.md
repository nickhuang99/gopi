## What the CER numbers mean

The reference is a **written** transcript: one character of it does not carry a timestamp, so its
position is only known to the accuracy of the archive's own characters-per-second rate
(1138 chars / 219.977 s = **5.1733 chars/s**). The bare invocation records **10 s**
(`voiceLocalDefaultSeconds`), so the transcript can only be a slice of the reference, and the score
depends on the slice the reader chooses. Both readings are reported, each with its rule:

| reference | rule | hyp / ref chars | edit distance | **CER** |
|---|---|---|---|---|
| `fixture/ref60.txt` (the 60 s crop, the deliverable's own reference) | score the transcript against the whole crop | 41 / 276 | 236 | **85.51 %** |
| `refwin.txt` (window-matched) | `ref60[2 : 2+50]` — anchor the transcript's own first matched character, then the 10 s window (52 chars at 5.1733 chars/s) | 41 / 47 | 7 | **14.89 %** |

The window-matched 7 edits decompose exactly (`difflib` opcodes, normalised text):
**1 substitution** (`续→序` — the engine's own error, the same one the design run recorded) and a
**6-character trailing delete** at the window boundary — the transcript stops at reference char 43
of 60 s, i.e. it covers **7.93 s of the reference inside the 10.0 s window** (the rest is the
~0.2 s playback ramp-up plus the engine's own latency), of which the first 5 characters are the
fixture's own fade-in. So the 14.89 % is **1 engine error in 47 characters, the other 6 edits being
window/pad boundary**.
The 85.51 % figure is NOT an engine-accuracy claim: it is what any whole-crop denominator reports for
a bounded window (the same effect, at the same order, as the 21.28 % the design run reports for its
10 s window — see `devdoc/voice-input-default-shape.md`).
